polartools.dichro_maps#

Shared helpers for dichroism difference maps from flyscan pairs.

A dichro measurement is a pair of flyscans taken at the two phase-plate settings; the map of interest is the difference between them. These functions find the pairs in a scan range, put the two members on a common grid, and plot a series of the resulting maps.

Factored out of data/experiment_plots.ipynb so several notebooks can share one copy. The reduction itself lives in process_flyscan.

polartools.dichro_maps.baseline_value(scan, key, folder)[source]#

One signal from a scan’s baseline stream, or None if unavailable.

The baseline is the trustworthy source for per-scan device values. The diffractometers metadata blob also carries a mono energy, but it can be stale – it read 6.200 keV for a whole energy series – so labels must not come from there.

polartools.dichro_maps.find_pairs(scans, label_key, folder, require_positions=True, atol=0.0)[source]#

Consecutive scans sharing a label value but differing in phase plate.

Returns [(label, scan_a, scan_b), ...] with scan_a the lower number. Pairs without a position stream are dropped unless asked for.

atol is how far the label may differ between the two members and still count as one setting. Motor readbacks jitter – huber_hp_z gives 0.0500 for one member and 0.0501 for the other – so those series need a tolerance, well below the spacing between settings. The default of 0 means exact equality, right for setpoint-valued series.

polartools.dichro_maps.normalised_map(scan, roi, folder, batch=200)[source]#

(x, y, z/I0) for one flyscan.

Detector frame k is exposed during the position samples carrying eigerTrig k+1, and groups_for_frames matches them by that trigger value, so a frame whose trigger group is missing drops out instead of shifting every later frame along by one.

polartools.dichro_maps.absorbance_map(scan, roi, folder, batch=200, reference='open', open_pct=99.0)[source]#

(x, y, mu*t) for one flyscan.

reference="open" (default) gives true absorbance, mu*t = ln(I_open / I): zero where the beam is clear, rising to the sample’s own absorbance. I_open is the open_pct percentile of I/I0 over the scan, which is the unobstructed level provided the scan actually runs off the sample at one end – if it does not, the reference lands on the thinnest part of the sample and every value is offset low.

reference="i0" uses ln(I0 / I) literally. I is a detector sum and I0 a monitor counter, so that carries a constant ln(gain) offset (about -11.9 in the open beam here).

The offset cancels in a CL - CR difference either way, but not in their sum, so anything normalising by mu_L t + mu_R t needs the open reference to mean anything.

Absorbance, not transmission, is what is linear in path length, so this is the quantity a tomographic reconstruction wants.

polartools.dichro_maps.dichroic_ratio(diff, mean, floor_frac=0.1)[source]#

(mu_L t - mu_R t) / (mu_L t + mu_R t) from a difference and mean.

The denominator is twice the mean absorbance, which goes to zero wherever the beam is clear – the ratio is 0/0 in the open beam and would amplify noise without bound there. Points whose sum falls below floor_frac of its 99th percentile are masked out instead of returned as huge numbers.

Being a ratio of absorbances it divides out the sample thickness, so it measures the magnetisation itself rather than magnetisation x path length.

polartools.dichro_maps.pair_difference(scan_a, scan_b, roi, folder, grid_n=200, quantity='transmission')[source]#

Regrid both scans of a pair and return (XG, YG, difference, mean).

The two scans don’t visit the same points – the stage free-runs – so each is interpolated onto a common grid before subtracting. The grid spans only the overlap of the two scans’ travel, so neither is extrapolated.

quantity selects what is differenced: "transmission" gives I/I0, "absorbance" gives mu*t = ln(I0/I). The transform is applied per point before interpolating, because a log of an interpolated value is not the interpolation of the logs.

polartools.dichro_maps.average_pairs(pairs, roi, folder, grid_n=200)[source]#

Average several dichro pairs measured at the same position.

pairs is [(scan_a, scan_b), ...]. Every pair is put on one common grid – the intersection of all their travel – before differencing, since each is otherwise regridded to its own overlap and they share no sample points. Each pair’s own common-mode offset is removed first: those differ from pair to pair and would bias the mean.

Returns (XG, YG, mean_difference, mean_transmission, stack), where stack holds the individual offset-removed differences so the caller can see how far the repeats agree.

polartools.dichro_maps.averaged_series(groups, roi, folder, grid_n=200, verbose=True)[source]#

Build plottable entries from [(title, [(a, b), ...]), ...].

One entry per group, holding the mean of that group’s repeats. The offsets are already removed inside average_pairs(), so offset is zero.

polartools.dichro_maps.build_series(pairs, roi, folder, grid_n=200, verbose=True)[source]#

Difference map for every pair, ordered by the series parameter.

Sorted by label rather than scan number: settings are not always visited in order, and a trend drawn in acquisition order would double back on itself. A pair that fails to load – a scan still being written – is skipped rather than losing the ones that did.

polartools.dichro_maps.plot_difference_series(series, title, label_fmt='{:.3f}', ncol=3, clip=99.0, share=True)[source]#

Difference maps on one shared symmetric scale, common offsets removed.

The two phase-plate settings don’t transmit equally, so every difference sits on a large constant offset that would swamp the structure. It is instrumental, so it is subtracted per panel.

share links the panel axes, which is what makes a series comparable point to point. Turn it off when the members sit at different places on the sample: shared limits would then span the whole excursion and squash every map into a sliver.

polartools.dichro_maps.plot_stitched(series, title, label_fmt='{:.4f}', clip=99.0, width=5.0, max_height=34.0, cmap='seismic')[source]#

Lay every map of a series at its own coordinates on a single axes.

For a set of maps taken at stepped sample positions: rather than one panel each, they are drawn where they actually sit, so the strip reads as one picture of the sample. The tiles are positioned by the interferometer coordinates, which do follow the coarse stage – a 10 um move in huber_hp_y shows up as about +10000 here.

Each tile still has its own common-mode offset removed. Without that the instrumental offset, which differs from pair to pair, would put a visible step at every tile boundary.

The default colour map is seismic rather than the RdBu_r used for panel figures. Most of a dichroic difference sits well inside the clipped range – the median deviation is under a third of the 99th percentile – and RdBu_r spends that whole span near white, which washes the domains out. seismic changes lightness about four times faster at zero, so the structure carries. It saturates harder at the ends, which also makes per-scan artefact bands obvious.

polartools.dichro_maps.write_gif(series, path, title, label_fmt='{:.1f}', clip=99.0, fps=2.5, cmap='RdBu_r', width=5.4, pingpong=True, dpi=100, scale='frame')[source]#

Animate a series of difference maps as a GIF, one frame per member.

All frames share one colour scale and one set of axis limits, so what moves between frames is the data and not the rendering. pingpong plays the sweep forwards then backwards, which avoids the jump a looping monotonic sweep otherwise makes when it wraps from the last setting back to the first.

The writer is driven directly rather than through FuncAnimation: that calls the draw function an extra time for setup, and the correspondence between requested frames and grabbed images is then not one to one – the first angle of a sweep goes missing. Here one explicit grab follows each draw.

Returns the path written.

polartools.dichro_maps.line_cut(entry, y0, robust=8.0)[source]#

One row of a map at the y nearest y0.

Returns (y_actual, x, i_a, i_b, mean, diff, kept). The two polarisations are recovered from the stored mean and difference (diff = i_b - i_a), so no re-read is needed.

griddata leaves a few wild values on the rim of the interpolated area – a single such column sits at the left edge of the #778-781 map, where the difference reads -12709 against a row median of -35. They are dropped here rather than allowed to dominate the derivative; kept is the mask.

polartools.dichro_maps.peak_fwhm(x, y, k)[source]#

Full width at half maximum of the peak of y at index k.

Walks out to each side until the curve falls through half the peak height and interpolates the crossing. Returns (fwhm, x_lo, x_hi), with NaNs if the peak does not come back down inside the cut.

polartools.dichro_maps.plot_line_cut(entry, y0, title=None, window=11, figsize=(11, 3.3), clip=99.0)[source]#

Map, the dichroic difference along one row, and its x-derivative.

The derivative peaks where the difference crosses between domains, so its extrema locate the walls and their width sets an upper bound on the probe size convolved with the true wall width.

polartools.dichro_maps.edge_position(x, y, window=11)[source]#

Sub-pixel x of the steepest point of a step in y(x).

The derivative peak is located on the smoothed curve, then refined by a parabola through the peak sample and its two neighbours, so the result is not quantised to the 50 nm grid step.

Returns (x_edge, slope); slope carries the sign of the step.

polartools.dichro_maps.align_series(series, method='map', y0=None, step=50.0, reference=None, max_shift=4000.0)[source]#

Line frames up in x, undoing the xeryon stage runout.

Rotating the xeryon translates the sample sideways, so the frames of an animation jump about. Each frame is registered against a reference frame and its x coordinates offset by the measured displacement. Only the coordinates move – the data are never resampled, so no interpolation blur is added.

method="map" cross-correlates the whole difference map, method="cut" only the row nearest y0. The map is the safer default: it uses every pixel, and it also measures the vertical component, which is the assumption the correction rests on. For scans 807-878 that assumption holds well – dy rms 34-36 nm against dx rms 326-373 nm.

The reference defaults to the middle frame, which correlates better with both ends of a sweep than either end does with the other. max_shift leaves a frame alone if its measured displacement is implausibly large.

Returns (aligned_series, report).

polartools.dichro_maps.overlap_report(tile_a, tile_b, field='diff')[source]#

Compare two x-adjacent tiles where their travel overlaps.

The overlap is the one place the same sample is measured twice, so it is the only direct check that the two tiles agree – on level, and on whether they are placed correctly relative to each other.

Returns a dict with the overlap span, the median b - a offset, and the correlation between the two copies. A high correlation with a non-zero offset means the tiles are aligned but sit on different baselines; a low correlation means the x placement itself is suspect.

polartools.dichro_maps.stitch_x(tiles, fields=('diff', 'mean'), step=None, seam=None, level_match=False)[source]#

Join x-adjacent tiles into one map, using each point only once.

Tiles are placed by their own interferometer coordinates – nothing is fitted or slid – and the duplicated strip where two tiles overlap is resolved by cutting at its midpoint, so each tile contributes the half nearer its own centre and no sample point is counted twice.

seam overrides the cut position (a list of x values, one per gap). level_match shifts each tile after the first by the median difference measured in its overlap with the previous one; off by default, because a step at the seam is usually worth seeing rather than hiding.

Returns (XG, YG, {field: array}, info).

polartools.dichro_maps.interior_columns(x, lo_frac=1.0 / 3.0, hi_frac=2.0 / 3.0)[source]#

Columns in the central band of a scan – by default its middle third.

Used to set colour limits from the sample’s interior rather than from its absorption edges, where a sub-pixel CR/CL position mismatch across a large intensity step makes a difference many times any real dichroism (8.5x on the #946-949 tiles). Scaling to the whole map therefore renders the interior flat, hiding the domains.

The middle third is interior by construction: the sample is centred in the scan, which is what the short absorption alignment scan before each angle establishes. Taking a fixed fraction is deliberate – it clips the same way on every scan, where locating the edges from the intensity shifts with the contrast and can creep back towards them on a weakly absorbing sample.

Returns a boolean mask over x.

polartools.dichro_maps.shift_rows(image, x, dx)[source]#

Shift each row of image along x by dx (scalar or per row).

Positive dx moves the row’s content towards +x. Points shifted in from beyond the measured range become NaN rather than being extrapolated.

polartools.dichro_maps.line_shifts(ref, moving, x, max_shift=1500.0, min_points=40)[source]#

Per-row displacement of moving relative to ref, in x.

One normalised cross-correlation per row, refined by a parabola through the peak. Normalising matters: a plain dot product shrinks as the overlap shrinks with lag, so it always peaks at zero and reports no shift at all.

Rows with too little overlap give NaN, which shift_rows() treats as “leave this row alone”.

polartools.dichro_maps.realign_pair(diff, mean, x, mode='line', manual=0.0, max_shift=1500.0, shifts=None)[source]#

Re-derive a pair’s difference with the two polarisations aligned in x.

Takes the difference and mean a pair was already reduced to – the two polarisations are recovered exactly as mean -/+ diff/2 – so this needs no re-reading of the scans and can be re-run interactively.

mode is "none", "global" (one shift for the whole map, the median of the per-line measurements) or "line". manual adds a fixed offset in nm on top, for tuning by eye.

Returns (diff, mean, shifts).

polartools.dichro_maps.tile_pairs(pairs, roi, folder, grid_n=200, open_pct=99.0, open_level=None)[source]#

Reduce several CR/CL pairs against one open-beam reference.

Referencing each scan to its own high percentile treats the datasets differently, in two ways that both corrupt a stitch:

  • Within a pair, the difference picks up ln(ref_CL / ref_CR) – a constant that has nothing to do with dichroism and differs from tile to tile. On #1959-1962 that alone put the two tiles at -0.0053 and +0.0007.

  • Across tiles, a scan that never runs off the sample references itself to its own thinnest point and reports every absorbance too low by a constant. #1959 peaked at 2648 where #1961 plateaued at 3651, so tile A came out 0.349 low throughout – about 12%.

Transmission cannot exceed the unobstructed beam, so the largest percentile across all the scans is the best available estimate of it, and using that one value for every scan makes the tiles directly comparable: the reference cancels exactly in every difference, and absorbances share a zero. Pass open_level to override it.

Each scan is read once, however many pairs mention it. Returns (tiles, open_level); each tile carries XG, YG, diff, mean, a, b.

polartools.dichro_maps.common_y_range(first_scans, folder, group=4)[source]#

The y interferometer range shared by several angle groups.

The stage drifts in y over a long tomographic series – about 1.56 µm between #1716 (25°) and #2479 (203.8°) – so the slice measured at one angle is not the slice measured at another. Only the overlap of every angle can be reconstructed; outside it, different angles are looking at different parts of the sample.

Reads position streams only, never detector frames, so it is cheap. Pass the first scan of each group; group scans are examined from each. Returns (lo, hi), or None if no scan could be read.
