polartools.spectrum_viewer#

A fluorescence-spectrum viewer for the Vortex files beside a master file.

The softglue (“sg”) edition. Identical to spectrum_viewer apart from what the “Combine” box does and what the monitor list offers – see The sgz layout below. It is a copy and not a subclass because almost every change is inside a long method rather than at a seam one could override, and because the original has to keep running untouched while this one is tried on live data.

The multi-element silicon-drift detector writes its MCA spectra the way the area detectors write their frames: not into *_master.hdf at all, but into a file of its own in a subdirectory of the master’s, listed in entry/instrument/bluesky/metadata/detectors_file_relative_path:

vortex: vortex/scan_000055.h5

The cube inside is entry/data/data, shaped (frames, elements, bins):

entry/data/data   (800, 7, 4096)  uint32, gzip, chunks (1, 7, 4096)

Two things about that shape are not constants and must be read from the file. The element count is one: /home/beams/POLAR/fluo_data/vortex/scan_000122.h5, from the 2025-1 run, is (240, 4, 4096) and uint16 with no compression. The dead-time bookkeeping is the other, and it is worse than a shape change – the names and the channel numbering differ between the two vintages:

2026-3   entry/instrument/NDAttributes/CHAN1DTFactor .. CHAN7DTFactor   1-based
2025-1   entry/instrument/NDAttributes/ICR_0/OCR_0   .. ICR_3/OCR_3     0-based

so both are probed for, in that order, and a file carrying neither simply does not offer the correction. corrected = raw * DTFactor is the XIA convention and is confirmed rather than assumed here: (1 - 1/DTFactor) * 100 reproduces the CHAN{n}DTPercent the file records beside it to within rounding (1.0368 against 3.5448%). Where only ICR and OCR are recorded the same factor is ICR / OCR.

Why the correction is on by default, against this package’s general habit of showing what was recorded. On scan 55 element 3 runs 8 to 33 percent dead while element 6 runs 0.2 percent. Draw seven raw curves in a window whose whole purpose is comparing elements and the plot says “element 3 is weak”, which is a wrong conclusion about the hardware stated confidently. The checkbox turns it off; the default is the one that is not misleading.

Finding the file. The recorded path is tried second, which is the reverse of image_viewer.image_path_for_master() and needs saying why. In 2026-3/Jeremy_26-3/data/DefaultSample there are 97 masters and 66 vortex files, and the recorded path resolves for 14 of them. From scan 76 the IOC’s file template drifted out of step with the writer:

master says   vortex/scan76.hdf5
disk holds    vortex/scan_000076.h5

for 52 masters, with detectors_file_full_path carrying the same wrong name. The <master dir>/vortex/<stem minus "_master">.h5 convention resolves all 66. So the convention leads and the recorded line is the fallback, for the writers that do not follow it. Both are resolved against the master’s own directory for the reason image_viewer.image_path_for_master() gives: an absolute path does not merely fail after the data is copied, it succeeds, opening the original instead of the copy that was clicked.

The cube is never loaded whole. 8000 frames is 917 MB as uint32, and several scans overlay in one window. Worse, re-reading it is not something a frame selector can do per keystroke: it is gzip-compressed, so inflating it costs ~1.3 s however it is sliced (64, 256 or 1024 frames at a time, or in one bulk read – all within 10% of each other, because the inflate is the cost and not the I/O).

So one streamed pass at open builds a cumulative sum per block of frames: with B = ceil(n_frames / PYRAMID_BLOCKS), _cum[i] is the sum of everything before block i. Any frame range is then _cum[j] - _cum[i] plus at most two short ragged ends. Measured on the 8000-frame worst case: building both pyramids 2.2 s against 1.4 s for a plain total – near enough free – 29 MB held per pyramid instead of 917 MB, and an arbitrary range answered in 81 us instead of 1.3 s. That is what makes dragging the frame spin boxes possible at all.

The accumulator is float64, deliberately and not idly: numpy promotes a uint32 sum to uint64, and int64 += uint64 resolves to float64, which += then refuses under the same_kind casting rule. An int64 accumulator raises UFuncTypeError on the first block. float64 takes any source dtype without thinking, is exact for integers to 2**53 against a maximum bin count here of 2.4e6, and is what dead-time correction and the plot both want anyway.

The energy axis is nominal. Nothing in either file records a calibration. The defaults here (10 eV/bin, 0 eV offset) come from reading known lines off scan 55 – Fe Ka at bin 635, the elastic peak at bin 1117, consistent over a 5 keV span – so they are close, but they are an inference and the axis says so. Type over them when the real gain is known.

Frames are not scan points, but the detector records which is which. Division is the wrong instrument, and scan 125 shows why: it declares num_points 100, was aborted after 9, and still wrote 800 frames. Divide by the declaration and the note reads a tidy “8 frames per point”, which is wrong and does not look wrong; divide by the 9 rows the primary stream really holds and the ratio is 88.9. So the count is read from the stream, never from the metadata. The per-frame timestamps cannot rescue the mapping either – NDArrayEpicsTSSec and nSec are all zero in these files, so image_viewer’s nearest_index/_scan_time_base machinery has nothing to work from.

What does rescue it is ArrayCounter, which counts frames within a point and resets to 1 at every one, so cumsum(counter == 1) - 1 is an exact frame-to-point map however ragged the scan – see SpectrumData.point_index(). On scan 125 it finds ten complete 80-frame blocks against the 9 rows the scan recorded: the detector finished a point the scan was aborted before writing down. So anything pairing frames with stream rows takes the shorter of the two and says so. The 2025-1 vintage records no ArrayCounter and is 1:1 anyway, where even division stands in.

The second pane answers the question the first one cannot: how the intensity in one emission line moves as the scan proceeds. It is off until “Plot ROI” is pressed, and then takes a third of the width – off because it costs a pass over the cube that a window opened to look at a spectrum should not pay for unasked.

The region of interest is a centre in keV and a half-width in eV, shown as the grey band on the spectrum and draggable there. It opens on the tallest bin of everything loaded, one default half-width either side; “Peak” re-centres it on the tallest bin of what is drawn inside the bins shown, which is how you put it on a line after zooming to one. Be clear about what the opening guess finds: on these files the tallest peak is the elastic line at the incident energy, 11.20 keV, not a fluorescence line – Fe Ka on scan 55 is 71 percent of it – and the elements do not even agree, element 1 peaking at bin 635 where the sum peaks at 1120.

Held as an energy and not as bins, which is the whole reason it is in keV: correct the nominal gain below and the ROI stays on the energy it names, so a band that walks away from a peak is telling you the calibration you typed is wrong. The price is that a gain edit now moves the derived bins, so it re-reads the cube where before it only relabelled an axis.

Beside the spectrum, then, one curve per element – never a sum or a mean across them, since which element sees a line is the comparison this pane exists to make – each the mean of the frames on show at each scan point, and then the mean of the scans on show. Repeated scans are the thing worth averaging; overlaying them instead would put seven curves times three scans on one axis. A point the frame range only partly covers is a mean over fewer frames, which is correct and is counted in the status line, and a point it misses entirely is NaN rather than zero. The x axis defaults to the field the scan’s own hints nominate (energy here, Samp_x on the lup scans), which is also why it is read from the master rather than assumed: the motor scans record no energy column at all. Moving the band costs one streamed pass over the cube (~1 s on 8000 frames), cached on the bin pair, so every element tick, dead-time toggle, frame range and axis change afterwards replots from memory.

The monitor the trend is divided by is chosen by liveness, not by name. Every 2026-3 scan here records bI0 as identically zero while gI0 and hI0 run, so a picker that went down a fixed list of names would open on a divisor of zero and show a blank plot. A candidate is offered only where every loaded scan records at least one positive reading of it – see SpectrumViewerWindow._usable_monitors().

The sgz layout. The softglue scans write more than one vortex frame per scan point – 88 of them on the qxscan runs this was written for – and those frames alternate, because the softglue toggles between two states frame by frame. Measured on scan 597, point 0, totalled over all elements:

14830 16614 15031 16543 15007 16831 14920 16457 14953 16555 14677 16762
odd  (files 1,3,5..) 14827.9 +- 192.7     even (2,4,6..) 16528.8 +- 203.0
asymmetry -5.42%; of 400 random 44/44 splits of these same frames the
widest apart was 548 and the median 112

1701 counts apart with 200 of scatter inside each half, against a shuffle that never managed a third of it: the split is the measurement, not noise. Every element shows it, from -2.6% on element 1 to -8.5% on element 5, and the whole 62-point scan comes to -4.83% (-4.88% dead-time corrected; every number here is raw counts). So where a scan has more than one frame per point the Combine box offers their difference as well as their sum – odd - even, numbering the frames within their point from 1, which is the convention that fixes the sign and is repeated on every label that carries it. ArrayCounter gives that numbering exactly, running 1..88 and restarting at each point; where a file lacks it, position within the point stands in. “Odd only” and “Even only” are there because a difference is only believable once both halves have been looked at.

This is also what vortex_total_el1 and vortex_total_el7 are doing in the monitor list, where spectrum_viewer offers nothing of the kind. Both are computed from the cube rather than read from the master, which is what lets them differ between the odd frames and the even ones – a recorded channel has one reading per scan point and cannot. Dividing the sum by one of them is still not normalisation: it is one region of this spectrum over another, and it flattens the very trend being looked for. Dividing the difference is the asymmetry, which is the quantity an XMCD measurement is for. So they are offered, last, where a click can reach them, and never chosen for you: gI0 and hI0 still open these scans.

Run standalone on one or more spectrum files, or let scan_viewer_sgz’s “Load Spectrum…” button open them for the scans it lists.

polartools.spectrum_viewer.spectrum_path_for_master(master: pathlib.Path) → pathlib.Path | None#

The spectrum file belonging to master, or None if none was found.

The <master dir>/vortex/<stem minus "_master">.h5 convention is tried first and the path the master records only second – the reverse of image_viewer.image_path_for_master(), because here the recorded value is wrong far more often than it is right (52 of 66 in the 2026-3 run; see the module docstring). Everything is resolved against the master’s own directory either way.

Two things keep the cost of that second chance off the common case, because reading the recorded value means opening the master with h5py and this function is asked of every row of the listing. The subdirectory is stat-ed first: a run that took no fluorescence at all has no vortex/, so 1254 TiSe2 masters answer “no spectrum” on one stat each instead of on an open each – 5.9 s of a listing refresh that was being spent to learn nothing. And the convention candidates are tested as they are built rather than collected first, so the 67 scans that do have a spectrum never open the master either. Only a master with a vortex/ beside it and no file of the expected name in it is worth asking what it thinks it wrote.

The stat gate is also the column’s definition rather than merely an optimisation: “a spectrum in subdirectory vortex” is the question being answered, so no vortex/, no tick, whatever the master records.

polartools.spectrum_viewer.resolve_spectrum_file(path: pathlib.Path) → pathlib.Path | None#

The spectrum file to open for path, master or spectrum file.

Deliberately a stat and not an open. The browser calls this once per row per refresh, and over the real mount opening each candidate to check its dataset costs 6.2 ms against 7 us for Path.is_file() – 0.6 s added to a listing of 97 scans, and far worse cold over NFS. So the column’s tick means “the file is there”, and whether it holds a cube this viewer can read is settled by SpectrumData, which raises. That is the same division image_viewer.image_path_for_master() makes, and for the same reason.

A file selected directly is the exception: nothing else would resolve it, and there is one of it rather than a listing’s worth. A master is never that exception, and the test below says so explicitly, because .hdf is one of SPECTRUM_SUFFIXES and the masters are *_master.hdf. Without the _master test every master that did not resolve would be opened to be asked whether it is itself a cube – 4.4 ms a row across 1254 TiSe2 masters, every one of which answers no. It would also be the one way this function could put a tick on a row that has no spectrum, since an image master with a 3-D entry/data/data would answer yes.

polartools.spectrum_viewer.is_multi_frame_scan(spectrum: pathlib.Path) → bool#

Whether a cube holds more than one frame per scan point.

The sgz softglue layout, and the one condition under which this edition’s extra features mean anything: with one frame per point there are no two halves for the Combine box to tell apart.

One open, and it reads the ArrayCounter and nothing else – a vector of small ints beside a cube of hundreds of megabytes. That is what makes it affordable once per row of a directory listing, where constructing a SpectrumData is not: that builds the whole summary pyramid, which is a streamed pass over every frame.

The answer is SpectrumData.multi_frame’s, computed the same way – the median point’s frame count, so that an aborted scan’s short trailing point does not speak for the scan. False for the 2025-1 vintage, which records no counter and is one frame per point throughout, and False for anything unreadable: this decides a colour, and a colour is not worth an exception.

polartools.spectrum_viewer.infer_bin_range(total: numpy.ndarray) → tuple[int, int]#

The bin range worth opening on, from where the counts actually are.

total is one spectrum summed over every frame and element. The range returned holds BIN_MASS_QUANTILE of its counts, centred by taking the same tail off each end, then padded by BIN_RANGE_MARGIN.

Counts and not peak height, because the two other rules that suggest themselves were measured against real files and neither survived. A threshold at a fixed fraction of the maximum depends on how sharp the tallest peak happens to be: at 1% of peak it gives bins 0-1173 on scan 55 but 0-2240 on scan 76, whose pileup peak at twice the elastic energy clears the bar, and a useless 292-397 on the 4-element file, which is one narrow line. A noise-floor rule – the last bin standing clear of the median of the dead high end – is worse still, returning 0-3400 for every 2026 file, because those carry a real low-level continuum all the way out and so the floor is never reached. Count mass is indifferent to both: 97 to 98 percent of every 2026 file’s counts sit below the elastic peak and 99.9 percent below the pileup peak, and the 4-element file concentrates at 297-662. One rule, five files, five sensible answers.

The last bin is dropped before anything is measured. It is not a spectrum bin but the accumulator everything above the ADC range piles into, holding 17454 counts on scan 55 while bins 4090-4094 hold none, so leaving it in anchors every inferred range to the right-hand edge of the axis.

polartools.spectrum_viewer.stack_mean(pieces: list[numpy.ndarray]) → numpy.ndarray#

Mean of several 1-D arrays of unequal length, padding with NaN.

The same function, and the same reasoning, as scan_viewer.stack_mean. It is copied rather than imported because scan_viewer imports this module, so the dependency cannot run the other way.

Row by row, never by interpolation: repeated scans step the same points, and one stopped part way is then simply short. A short scan leaves NaN where it did not reach, which is not the same as zero – the mean over the tail is over the scans that got there, and a point no scan reached comes back NaN and is left off the plot.

polartools.spectrum_viewer.write_aligned_columns(path: pathlib.Path, names: list[str], columns: list[numpy.ndarray], header: list[str]) → None#

Write columns side by side, space-aligned, under a commented header.

scan_viewer.write_columns written for whitespace instead of CSV, because the beamline’s own .dat files are SPEC-shaped – a # header, an #L line of column names, space-separated numbers – and a file dropped beside them should read the same way. Every name here is one word, which is what lets the names be whitespace-separated at all.

Each column is padded to the wider of its name and its widest value, so the table lines up for an eye as well as for numpy.loadtxt().

Columns of different lengths are squared off with blanks at the bottom. A value that is NaN is written nan instead: a curve normalized by a monitor that read zero has real holes in it, and “nothing was measured here” must not look like “this curve stopped earlier”.

polartools.spectrum_viewer.same_grid(first: numpy.ndarray, second: numpy.ndarray) → bool#

Whether two X columns step the same points, over the length they share.

SAME_X_STEPS of a step is the tolerance. Shaped after scan_viewer.same_grid; the point of it here is only to decide whether averaging the two is honest, so a disagreement is reported rather than acted on.

polartools.spectrum_viewer.apply_bold_defaults(app) → None#

Bold text everywhere this edition draws: Qt widgets and plots alike.

Both halves are needed and neither reaches the other – QApplication.setFont knows nothing about matplotlib, and matplotlib’s rcParams know nothing about Qt. Called at application start rather than at import, so that importing this module for a test leaves the process’s fonts alone.

axes.labelweight and axes.titleweight are set as well as font.weight: matplotlib keeps separate weights for those two and they do not follow the general one, so without them the axis labels come back normal under bold ticks.

One MCA cube, summarised so that any frame range is cheap to total.

Never holds the cube. What it holds is a cumulative sum per block of frames, raw and dead-time-corrected, each (n_blocks + 1, n_elements, n_bins) float64 – see the module docstring for the measurements that shape this.

Whether this scan took more than one vortex frame per point.

The sgz layout, and the one condition under which the odd/even contrasts mean anything: with one frame per point there are no two halves to tell apart.

Judged on the median point rather than the smallest. An aborted scan leaves a short trailing point – scan 125 writes ten 80-frame blocks against the nine rows its stream recorded – and that one ragged end says nothing about how the scan was run.

property odd_frames: numpy.ndarray | None#

Mask of the odd-numbered frames, counting from 1 within their point.

Frame 1 of a point is odd, frame 2 even, and so on – the user’s numbering, and the only thing that fixes the sign of a difference, so it is repeated on every label that carries one.

ArrayCounter answers exactly, being a 1-based position that restarts at each point (1..88 on the sgz scans). Where a file lacks it the position within an evenly divided point stands in, and where neither is available there is no numbering to speak of and this is None – which only happens on scans that are not multi_frame anyway.

roi_trend(lo: int, hi: int, slot: str = 'roi') → tuple[numpy.ndarray, numpy.ndarray | None]#

Counts in bins [lo, hi] per frame, as (n_frames, n_elements).

Raw and dead-time corrected, both from the one pass – the correction is a per-frame, per-element factor, constant across bins, so summing the bins first and scaling after is the same number as scaling each bin and summing, for a fraction of the arithmetic.

The pyramid cannot serve this: it is cumulative per block, so it has no frame interiors left to report. So this reads the cube, in READ_CHUNK blocks like _sum_slice() – 0.08 s for 800 frames, 1.0 s for 8000 – and the result is cached on (lo, hi). One array answers every element selection and both dead-time variants, which is what keeps ticking an element off a redraw rather than a re-read.

slot is which cache the answer is kept in. Several callers want different ranges at the same time – the ROI band, and one band per computed monitor – and a single slot would make every redraw read the cube again for each of them. A slot each, rather than a dict of the last few ranges: a drag makes a new key per step, so an eviction policy would throw away the other bands’ arrays on every one of them. Nothing is evicted, so the cost is one such array per slot in use – 0.6 MB per slot on scan 597, and only for a monitor that has actually been divided by.

range_sum(first: int, last: int, dead_time: bool, part: str = 'all') → numpy.ndarray#

Total frames first to last inclusive, as (n_elements, n_bins).

Whole blocks come out of the pyramid by subtraction; the partial block at each end is read from the file. Clamped rather than refused, because one spin-box range is shared by scans of different length.

part picks which frames of each scan point are counted, numbering them from 1: "all", "odd", "even", or "difference" for odd minus even. Everything but "all" needs odd_frames, and the first such call builds _odd_pyramid(); after that they are all subtractions and cost what "all" costs.

class polartools.spectrum_viewer.MonitorRoiWindow(parent, monitor: str)#

Bases: PyQt6.QtWidgets.QDialog

Picks the energy window one computed monitor counts in.

Opened when a monitor from COMPUTED_MONITORS is chosen, because such a monitor is not a channel read from the master: it is one element of this cube, summed over a window, and a window has to be chosen somewhere. The master’s own vortex_total_roi1 is a fixed hardware region whose bounds these files record nowhere, so there is nothing to inherit and the choice has to be made here.

One instance per computed monitor, each holding its own band, rather than one that retitles itself when the monitor changes. A single instance would have to re-seed its two boxes on every switch, and those writes go through _set_range() while the viewer still holds the old monitor’s range – which is the value the guard there compares against, so the reseed would write one monitor’s band into the other’s. Two instances have no such path.

Non-modal, and it drives the parent’s redraw on every change: the way to use it is to drag the band and watch the trend next door move. The range outlives the window – closing it keeps what was picked – because it belongs to the viewer, not to this dialog.

Shows its own element alone, over the frames the main window is showing, and on a log axis by default: picking a region out of a fluorescence spectrum means seeing the three decades under the line, which is exactly what a linear axis hides.
